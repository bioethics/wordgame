What changes, and what doesn't

  • The Market is a fair, not a sheet. A trestle of deal under a striped awning, laid over the bench; the section names are painted on the wood in the sheet's small capitals, and every price is a paper tag tied to the thing it prices.
  • The calling cards stay. They were already the best-looking thing in the shop — ivory, a rule tinted by rarity, a ribbon for the livery, foil for a postnom. Only the tag changes.
  • Sorts sit in a type case, the same sockets as the hand's case on the board, so a tile for sale looks like a tile you could own.
  • Stalls are signs hung from a rail, with the trade written under the name; the stall's own view opens beneath its awning as now.
  • The alley is the same trestle at night: a tarpaulin for the awning, one lantern, a black cloth, and chalk where the fair has type. Nothing there is a bargain, and the tags say so in red.
  • Not drawn here: the stall views (Gilder, Punchcutter, Dresser, Painter, Smelter, Stereotyper), the compost heap, the quire lot, the collection sheet, and the Colophon — each follows from these two once the idiom is agreed.
