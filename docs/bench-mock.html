What this bench argues

  1. The stick is a ruler. The length multiplier is engraved along the composing stick, so how far a word reaches is the measure — you read ×2½ off the scale under the seventh sort rather than off a chip.
  2. The page is the status. Chapter, folio, quota, words left and the words already set are one sheet of paper: the page you are filling. The quota inks in along its foot.
  3. The case shows the hand. One compartment per place, so a wider hand is more compartments, not a number somewhere.
  4. The proof sets the arithmetic in type. Points, then each seat in running order, then the measure and the paints — named, in order, and only the factors that apply.
  5. One surface, no panels. Objects on a bench instead of six rounded boxes; small capitals from the printed page instead of sans-serif micro-labels; the one loud thing is the red ink.

Everything here is a sketch of the board only: three patrons, no dictionary, one page. Tap a sort or type; ↵ prints.
