# The themed lists, read for words that don't belong (5 November)

Four lists were read whole: spooky (the Sexton), romantic (the Paramour), rude
(the Vulgarian) and nautical (the Seadog). Cute was read whole earlier the same
day, with the author's rulings (`MAKING-LISTS.md`), and was not read again.

The rule, in the author's words: the themes are meant to be interpreted very
broadly, using all senses of the theme word, and a word comes off only if, even
at a stretch and squinting a bit, it doesn't belong.

1. **Proposals.** Seven readers read every word of the four lists against a
   brief for each theme written to that rule, and proposed a word only where
   no sense of it touched the theme: 525 proposals.
2. **Rescue.** A fresh reader per list took that list's proposals and argued
   for keeping each one: any sense (technical, regional, slang, archaic), an
   idiom, a pun, an inflection of a word on the list, an association a player
   could make. A word with any case, however tenuous, was kept.
3. **A last read** of what was left to cut (by the session): three more kept
   (marked below). Then the lists were cut: **66 words**.

Every decision is below: the cuts with the proposer's reason and the rescue
reader's confirmation, then the 54 kept on a thin case (the first to
look at if a list should be firmer), then every other rescue with its case.
To put a cut word back, add its line to `wordlists/theme-<list>.txt`.

Also removed, as housekeeping: six entries the dictionary no longer holds,
since they were cut from it as junk on 5 November, so they could never score:
CRAYOLA, CRAYOLAS (cute), CONSES, CONSING, MADS (rude), ROBER (spooky).
